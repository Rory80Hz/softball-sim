# Slow-Pitch Play Simulator Functional Specification

Version: 1.0

This specification defines externally observable behavior for unit tests, integration tests, and manual acceptance checks.

## 1. Scope and Terms

- Field coordinates are measured in feet.
- Field coordinates use home plate as `(0, 0)` and positive `y` toward center field.
- A runner is an offensive player. The first runner in a play is the batter.
- A fielder is a defensive player.
- `active` means the object participates in simulation updates.
- A play may be `idle`, `playing`, or `paused`.
- While a play is `paused`, the user MAY direct players: select a fielder and click the ball or a field spot to send it there; if the fielder holds the ball, click another fielder to throw (success depends on throwing attributes); select a runner and click a valid base (a later base, or the last safe base) to send them. Directed fielders are no longer controlled by the AI.
- A ball may be `idle`, `hit`, `throw`, `loose`, `held`, or `dead`.

## 2. Player and Team Data

### 2.1 Player defaults

A newly created player MUST contain:

- `name`
- `position`
- `battingOrder`
- `number`
- `attributes` with values from 1 through 100:
  - `speed`
  - `throwSpeed`
  - `throwAccuracy`
  - `armStrength`
  - `catchAccuracy`
  - `battingContact`
  - `battingPower`

Default attributes MUST be 55 unless explicitly supplied.

`clampAttribute(value)` MUST return an integer in the inclusive range 1 through 100.

### 2.2 Teams

The application MUST initialize two teams when no saved teams exist:

- Home Sluggers
- Away Thunder

Each default team MUST contain ten players at these positions:

`P, C, 1B, 2B, 3B, SS, LF, CF, RF, SF`

Each player MUST have a unique ID. Each team MUST have a unique ID.

### 2.3 Persistence

Team/player data MUST be persisted to local storage after a store update.

Loading saved data MUST merge missing player attributes with the current defaults. This includes players saved before newer attributes were added.

A malformed or absent saved value MUST cause the application to use valid default teams rather than throw during initialization.

## 3. Field and Camera

### 3.1 Field geometry

The field MUST use the configured physical measurements consistently:

- Base distance: 65 feet
- Pitching distance: 50 feet
- Fence distance: 300 feet
- Home, first, second, and third base coordinates MUST form the configured diamond.

The renderer MUST preserve field proportions when the viewport is resized.

The configured scene boundary MUST include:

- 60 feet behind home plate
- 60 feet beyond the outfield fence
- Equivalent lateral limits around the foul-line area

The dark-green surrounding ground MUST stop at the same lateral and depth limits as the scene boundary.

Both foul lines MUST be visible. Two light-grey dead-ball lines MUST be visible, parallel to the foul lines and offset 15 feet into foul territory.

### 3.2 Camera controls

The user MUST be able to:

- Zoom in and out.
- Pan the view.
- Rotate the 3D view.
- Return to a top view.
- Select a 3D camera view.
- Use the on-screen orientation gizmo where supported.

The camera MUST never rotate below the field. The camera polar angle MUST remain above the ground plane.

## 4. Play Configuration

Play Setup MUST expose controls for:

- Hit type: `grounder`, `line_drive`, `blooper`, `flyball`, `pop_up`.
- Hit location/direction, including infield, left center, center, right center, and deep-field locations.
- Runners on base: all eight combinations of first, second, and third base occupancy, including empty bases and bases loaded.
- Batter selection from the batting team.

Changing any play configuration MUST rebuild the initial play state. The configured players MUST appear before play starts.

The Start action MUST reset the current configuration before starting a new play. Reset MUST restore the configured fielders, runners, and ball state.

## 5. Ball Physics

### 5.1 Hit generation

The selected hit type and location MUST define the intended trajectory and direction.

Batter statistics MUST introduce randomized variation:

- Higher `battingContact` reduces target-location variance.
- Lower `battingContact` increases target-location variance.
- Higher `battingPower` increases grounder and line-drive horizontal speed.

Batting speed outputs SHOULD remain within realistic softball ranges. With normal random variation, a maximum-rated batter SHOULD produce approximately:

- Line drives: 95-105 feet per second (about 65-72 mph).
- Grounders: 75-85 feet per second (about 51-58 mph).

The batting attributes represent an elite player at 100, not an impossible or superhuman result.

Hit speed MUST be generated by the hit type and batter power. Users do not directly configure hit speed.

Expected trajectory behavior:

- Grounder: starts near the ground and rolls.
- Line drive: fast, relatively flat trajectory.
- Blooper: short, moderate arc.
- Fly ball: deeper, higher arc.
- Pop-up: high arc with substantial hang time and relatively short travel.

Gravity MUST affect airborne hits. When an airborne ball reaches ground level, it MUST be marked landed and MUST NOT later be treated as an airborne catch.

A grounded ball MUST decelerate through ground friction. It MUST not travel forever at constant speed.

### 5.2 Ball visibility and ownership

The ball MUST be rendered as a smaller optic-yellow dot/sphere.

Only one fielder may own the ball at any moment.

- `held`: ball has a carrier and is not independently moving.
- `throw`: ball is moving from a thrower to a target.
- `loose`: ball is on the field and must be recovered.
- `dead`: play has ended because the ball left the playable area.

When a fielder throws, they MUST lose possession immediately. The ball MUST travel along its throw path. It MUST not respawn at the thrower or receiver.

A missed throw MUST become loose and MUST be recoverable by a fielder.

## 6. Throwing

Throw behavior MUST use the throwing fielder's attributes:

- `throwSpeed` controls throw travel time and arc characteristics.
- `throwAccuracy` controls target error.
- `armStrength`, combined with `throwSpeed`, controls maximum throw distance.

A throw beyond the fielder's maximum range MUST fail to reach the requested target.

A throw MUST follow a ballistic arc and land at approximately receiver height when it reaches its finite endpoint.

A base MUST never receive or catch a ball by itself. A throw is successfully received only when an active fielder is within receiving range of the destination.

## 7. Runners and Base Running

### 7.1 Movement

Each runner's movement speed MUST be derived from that runner's own `speed` attribute. Runners MUST move using elapsed time and MUST not accelerate merely because their target is updated.

A runner MUST follow base paths in order and may continue through all remaining bases to home.

After safely reaching a base, a runner SHOULD continue only when the next base is likely to be reached before the defense can cover it. Otherwise, the runner MUST hold at the safe base rather than blindly continuing.

When a runner reaches home:

- The runner is marked scored.
- The play score increments exactly once.
- The runner no longer participates in base-running movement.

### 7.2 Safe base state

Each runner MUST track:

- Current base.
- Last safely touched base.
- Bases already passed.
- Whether they scored.
- Whether they are out.

If a runner is cut off at a base and has not passed that base, they MUST attempt to return to their last safely touched base.

## 8. Outs and Rules

Only these out mechanisms are valid:

1. Caught airborne ball: the batter is out only if the ball is still airborne when caught.
2. Force out: a fielder with the ball touches/covers the forced base before the runner reaches or passes it.
3. Tag out: a fielder carrying the ball physically reaches the runner.

Bases cannot make outs without a fielder receiving the ball there.

A ground ball or any ball that has touched the ground MUST NOT retire the batter as a caught fly.

A runner who has passed a base MUST NOT be declared out at that base by a late force throw.

A non-force throw to a base MUST not itself record an out. It may cut off the runner and cause the runner to return to a safe base.

A tag attempt MUST require:

- An active runner.
- An active fielder.
- The fielder carrying the ball.
- The fielder reaching the runner within tag range.

Once a runner is out, they MUST stop moving and MUST not be counted as scored.

When all runners in the current play are out, the play MUST stop.

## 9. Defensive AI

### 9.1 General

Every fielder MUST use their own movement speed and position. AI decisions MUST be based on the ball's current or projected position, not only the configured hit target.

At most one fielder may be the primary caller/pursuer for a ball at a time. The caller SHOULD be the available fielder with the shortest estimated time to reach the ball or projected landing point. Estimated time MUST account for distance and that fielder's movement speed.

When the ball is secured, the defense SHOULD choose the easiest available out: a force play when a runner is forced and the base can be covered in time, otherwise a tag only when the ball-carrying fielder is already close enough; otherwise the defense should contain the runner at the next base.

### 9.2 Position responsibilities

- `C`: protect home and nearby foul territory.
- `P`: protect the pitching area and support the infield.
- `1B`: cover first base and right-side infield.
- `2B`: cover second base and right-middle infield.
- `3B`: cover third base and left-side infield.
- `SS`: cover second-base/left-middle infield and support second base.
- `SF`: cover shallow central/infield space.
- `LF`, `LC`, `CF`, `RC`, `RF`: pursue balls in their broad zones and provide backup/cutoff support.

Infielders MUST move to the exact relevant base when a runner is advancing and their position is responsible for that base.

### 9.3 Pursuit and backup

Outfielders MUST continue pursuing an airborne ball toward its continuously updated projected landing point until the ball lands or is caught.

After the ball lands, the primary available fielder MUST continue toward the ball's live position until possession or dead ball.

If an infielder misses a ground ball or playable landed ball, the nearest available outfielder MUST move inward and pursue the ball.

Non-pursuing outfielders MUST move toward useful, position-spaced backup or cutoff locations. They MUST not all collapse onto the same point, and they MUST not remain rigidly fixed in their starting zones when support is needed.

Manual movement targets, when implemented, MUST override automatic AI targets.

## 10. Events and Debug Ticker

The simulation SHOULD emit events for:

- Play started
- Fielder calls the ball
- Ball collected
- Throw started
- Tag attempt
- Runner cut off
- Run scored
- Runner out
- Ball dead
- Play stopped

The UI ticker MUST display recent events without blocking simulation. It SHOULD retain only a bounded number of recent messages.

## 11. Scoreboard

The scoreboard MUST display at minimum:

- Runs scored in the current play.
- Outs recorded in the current play.

Changing scenario configuration, resetting, or starting a fresh play MUST reset both values to zero.

## 12. Unit-Test Matrix

### Models and persistence

- Creates a player with all default attributes.
- Merges explicit attributes over defaults.
- Clamps values below 1, above 100, fractional values, invalid strings, and empty values.
- Creates two ten-player default teams with required positions.
- Loads malformed storage by falling back to defaults.
- Loads an old player record and supplies missing `armStrength`, `battingContact`, and `battingPower`.
- Persists updates through the store.

### Ball

- Hit types set the expected ball mode.
- Grounders begin landed and use friction.
- Airborne hits apply gravity and eventually set `hasLanded`.
- Landed balls cannot be classified as airborne catches.
- Throw uses speed to determine finite travel time.
- Throw uses accuracy to introduce endpoint error.
- Throw range limits endpoint distance.
- Throw has non-zero vertical velocity and updates height under gravity.
- Throw completion does not restore possession automatically.
- Missed throw can transition to loose state.

### Runner

- Runner movement distance equals speed multiplied by elapsed time, capped at remaining target distance.
- Runner reaches each configured base in order.
- Runner reaches home and emits exactly one score callback.
- Runner return-to-base clears forward movement.
- Runner marked out becomes inactive and cannot score.

### Engine rules

- `loadScenario` creates fielders at configured/default positions before start.
- `startPlay` starts the configured hit and runner paths.
- Random batter selection is from the batting team's roster.
- Selected batter stats reach hit generation.
- Only a fielder can trigger ball collection.
- Only a fielder can receive a throw.
- Force throw arriving before a runner records an out.
- Late force throw after a runner passes the base does not record an out.
- Non-force throw does not record an out by base arrival alone.
- Nearby ball-carrying fielder can tag a runner.
- Distant ball-carrying fielder chooses a base throw rather than a tag.
- All active runners return to last safe bases after a caught fly ball.
- All runners out transitions the engine to idle.
- Loose ball is pursued by a fielder and can become dead.
- Dead ball advances active runners exactly one base.
- Score and out counters emit the correct events.

### Defensive AI

- Caller selection uses distance divided by the individual fielder's movement speed.
- Caller changes if another fielder has a shorter ETA.
- Fielder position roles produce distinct base/zone targets.
- Infield miss activates nearest outfield recovery.
- Airborne pursuit continues while projected landing position changes.
- Backups move toward spaced cutoff positions.
- Fielders with possession do not receive new movement targets until they throw or release the ball.

### UI acceptance checks

- Play Setup exposes hit type, location, runner combination, and batter selectors.
- Changing any Play Setup selector resets visible players and score/out display.
- Start resets and runs the selected configuration.
- Reset restores the selected configuration without starting play.
- Player Config opens/closes and preserves its open state while changing team/player.
- Player name, number, and all attributes can be edited.
- Player edits affect the next play.
- Runner labels show player numbers.
- Fielder labels show position initials.
- Ticker displays recent simulation events.
- Camera controls respond to mouse/touchpad input and cannot rotate under the field.
- Dead-ball lines are visible and light grey.
